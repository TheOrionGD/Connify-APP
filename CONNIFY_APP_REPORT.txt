1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & PRODUCT VISION: SOCIAL COMMUNICATION INITIALIZATION
================================================================================

Connify is a zero-knowledge spatial social application designed to initialize real-world communication between two or more unknown persons (strangers) in physical proximity.

Traditional social discovery and networking apps require users to continuously broadcast unencrypted, precise GPS coordinates (latitude and longitude) to centralized cloud servers or expose public profiles to everyone in a geographic area. This traditional model creates severe privacy and safety risks, enabling unwanted tracking, harassment, or location profiling.

Connify fundamentally transforms spatial social interactions by serving as an anonymous initialization tool. It enables strangers co-located in the same venue, event, public space, or neighborhood to initialize real-time communication without ever compromising raw location privacy or exposing personal identity details prematurely.

Key System Highlights:
----------------------
1. Social Communication Initialization Between Strangers:
   Connify acts as an initialization bridge between two or more unknown individuals who are physically co-located. It enables strangers to discover shared interests, request physical co-presence, or initiate local conversations while maintaining total identity privacy until a mutual handshake is executed.

2. Zero-Knowledge Physical Co-Location:
   Requesters initiating a connection and nearby Peers are matched without either party or the central backend server ever transmitting, exposing, or storing raw geographic coordinates.

3. Hybrid Dual-Mode Network Topology:
   Operates seamlessly over cloud REST and WebSocket infrastructure when online. In scenarios with low cellular connectivity or crowded offline venues, the app falls back to local Wi-Fi mDNS discovery, direct TCP socket pairs, Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) ad-hoc mesh advertising, and encrypted SMS fallback.

4. Hardware-Backed Cryptographic Identity:
   Passwordless, anonymous identity architecture. User authentication relies on Ed25519/Curve25519 cryptographic keypairs generated inside hardware keychains (Android KeyStore / iOS Keychain) and verified using optional biometric authentication (TouchID, FaceID, Android Fingerprint).

5. Personal Safety & Trust Governance:
   Equipped with a multi-layered trust verification suite: Timed Safety Guard (check-in switch), high-decibel siren generator, realistic Fake Call simulator, community hazard map reporting, emergency helpline directories, and append-only cryptographic audit logs.

6. Cryptographic Auditability:
   Append-only hash-chained audit logging (prevHash -> entryHash) ensures all proximity connection episodes, helper matches, capsule issuances, and physical handshakes are immutably logged and mathematically verifiable.

3. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE & HYBRID NETWORK TOPOLOGY
================================================================================

Connify utilizes a resilient multi-tier network topology designed to guarantee spatial discovery regardless of internet availability or venue infrastructure state.

Primary Operational Modes:
--------------------------

Mode A: Cloud Relay Gateway (Standard Internet Connection)
- Clients communicate with the Fastify backend via HTTPS/TLS 1.3 REST endpoints.
- Persistent Socket.io connections are maintained over wss:// (WebSocket Secure).
- The central backend manages broad spatial room routing without ever receiving raw latitude/longitude coordinates.
- E2E encrypted messages and WebRTC SDP signaling packets are routed through Socket.io rooms.

Mode B: Local Network Peer-to-Peer (Wi-Fi Intranet / Local Venue Mesh)
- In scenarios where internet backhaul is weak or unavailable in crowded venues, clients employ mDNS / ZeroConf broadcast.
- Nearby devices discover peer IPs and establish direct TLS TCP/UDP socket connections.
- The SHARP proximity validation protocol executes directly between devices over the local socket without requiring cloud server mediation.

Mode C: Ad-Hoc Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) Mesh (Offline Proximity Discovery)
- When both cellular networks and local Wi-Fi infrastructure are absent, the app activates native BLE peripheral advertising and central scanning.
- Connection beacons containing compressed SHARP syndromes and device public keys are broadcast as GATT advertisement payloads.
- Nearby Connify instances act as BLE mesh nodes, relaying proximity beacons until they achieve direct physical co-location.

Mode D: Encrypted SMS Fallback
- For users without internet access, Connify formats connection request payloads into base64-encoded SMS messages dispatched directly to designated contacts or gateways.

4. THE SHARP PROTOCOL: ZERO-KNOWLEDGE PROXIMITY INITIALIZATION ENGINE
================================================================================

The SHARP Protocol (Selective Hashing and Blinded Proximity Protocol) is the custom-invented cryptographic core of Connify, fully documented in the protocol specifications workspace (sharp_protocol_spec.md, custom_invented_algorithm_code.txt, protocol_specification.txt). It solves the fundamental location-privacy vs GPS-accuracy paradox by operating over Galois Field arithmetic to achieve zero-knowledge spatial co-location verification between strangers.

Core Innovation & Algorithm Architecture:
------------------------------------------
1. Spatial Grid Quantization & Neighborhood Expansion:
   Raw sensor GPS coordinates (latitude, longitude) are truncated to 3 decimal places to create a base spatial cell:
     BaseCell = (floor(lat * 1000) / 1000, floor(lng * 1000) / 1000)
   To handle edge-boundary conditions where co-located strangers stand near adjacent cell borders, the engine automatically generates a 3x3 grid neighborhood matrix (9 spatial cells).

2. 1024-Bit Probabilistic Bloom Filter Vectorization:
   An empty 1024-bit vector B in {0, 1}^1024 is instantiated. Each of the 9 spatial cells C_k (k in [0..8]) is hashed 4 times using the FNV-1a (32-bit) algorithm with salt indices i in {0, 1, 2, 3}:
     hash_val = FNV1a(C_k || salt_i) mod 1024
     B[hash_val] = 1

3. Galois Field GF(2^4) Linear Partitioning:
   The 1024-bit Bloom filter vector B is segmented into 146 distinct 7-bit message blocks m_j in GF(2^7) (j in [0..145]):
     m_j = B[7*j .. 7*j + 6]

4. Systematic BCH(15,7) Code Syndrome Generation:
   Operating over the Galois Field GF(2^4) generated by the primitive polynomial p(x) = x^4 + x + 1 (0x13), each 7-bit block m_j is encoded using systematic generator polynomial G(x) = 0x1D1 (x^8 + x^7 + x^6 + x^4 + 1):
     c_j = (m_j << 8) XOR rem((m_j << 8), G(x))
   The lower 8 bits form the Parity Syndrome S_j. The raw 7-bit spatial message blocks m_j are discarded, and only the 146 parity syndromes S_j are transmitted over the wire. The central node receives zero raw geographic coordinates.

5. Blinded Grid HMAC Hashes:
   For final cryptographic verification, each spatial cell is concatenated with an ephemeral session key K_session and hashed via SHA-256:
     BlindedHash_k = SHA-256(K_session || C_k || Role)

6. Asymmetric Peer Reconstruction & Error Correction:
   A candidate peer receives the 146 parity syndromes S_j and blinded grid hashes. The peer generates its local 1024-bit Bloom filter from its current GPS location.
   For each block j, the peer combines its local 7-bit block with the received syndrome S_j:
     r_15 = (m'_j << 8) | S_j
   The peer executes a GF(2^4) Berlekamp-Massey & Chien search BCH decoder:
   - Power-sum syndromes S_1, S_2, S_3, S_4 are evaluated over GF(2^4).
   - Error-locator determinant Delta = (S_1 * S_3) XOR (S_2^2) is calculated.
   - If Delta != 0, two bit-errors caused by GPS sensor jitter are localized and corrected.
   - Upon error correction, the peer reconstructs the original Bloom filter and verifies co-location against the blinded hashes without exposing raw locations.

Performance & Benchmark Metrics (cpm_algorithm_analysis.xlsx):
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Syndrome Generation Time  : < 3.2 ms (Mobile Client JavaScript / TypeScript)
- Decoding Latency          : < 12.8 ms (146 blocks decoded over GF(2^4))
- Payload Network Footprint  : 146 bytes (Syndromes) + 288 bytes (Blinded Hashes) = 434 bytes total!
- False Positive Rate       : < 0.0001% (4-salt 1024-bit Bloom Filter)

7. REAL-TIME NETWORKING & SOCKET.IO PROTOCOL SPECIFICATION
================================================================================

Real-time communication is managed by Socket.io (backend/src/sockets/index.ts). It provides instant notification delivery, WebRTC signaling relay, and encrypted messaging.

Socket Event Catalog:
---------------------

1. Authentication & Rooms:
   - Event: "connection"
     Handles initial WebSocket handshake. Authenticates client using JWT token passed in connection parameters.
   - Event: "join_device"
     Subscribes socket to private room "device:<deviceId>" for targeted alert delivery.

2. Episode Lifecycle Signaling:
   - Event: "episode_created"
     Emitted by Requester. Server broadcasts connection metadata and SHARP syndromes to candidate peer rooms.
   - Event: "helper_matched"
     Emitted by Peer upon successful syndrome reconstruction. Server updates episode state to "matched" and alerts Requester.
   - Event: "episode_status_changed"
     Broadcasts status transitions ("pending" -> "matched" -> "active" -> "completed" -> "cancelled").

3. Encrypted Messaging & WebRTC Signaling:
   - Event: "send_message"
     Relays end-to-end encrypted chat messages between Requester and Peer.
   - Event: "webrtc_signal"
     Relays SDP offers, SDP answers, and ICE candidates to establish direct WebRTC audio calls.

4. Safety Guard Watchdog:
   - Event: "location_update"
     Periodically sends coarse spatial grid updates for active peers on patrol.
   - Event: "safety_guard_trigger"
     Fired automatically when a user fails to check in before their Timed Safety Guard timer expires.

13. BEHAVIORAL RISK ENGINE, TRUST SCORES & CRYPTOGRAPHIC AUDIT LOGS
================================================================================

To ensure system integrity and prevent malicious abuse or false alarms, Connify implements automated risk scoring and immutable audit chains:

1. Behavioral Risk Engine (backend/src/services/BehavioralRiskEngine.ts):
   Evaluates request lifecycles using multi-variable threat scoring:
     RiskScore = w1 * RateLimitViolation + w2 * VelocityAnomaly + w3 * DisputeHistory - w4 * TrustScore

   - Rate Limit Monitoring: Flag devices creating rapid consecutive connection requests.
   - Velocity Anomaly Detection: Calculates velocity between requests. Discards requests indicating impossible travel speeds (>150 km/h).
   - Reputation Adjustment: Prioritizes dispatch to peers with high verified trust scores.

2. Append-Only Cryptographic Audit Log Chain (AuditLog Model):
   Every critical system action (EPISODE_CREATED, CAPSULE_ISSUED, HANDSHAKE_COMPLETED, DISPUTE_RAISED) appends an entry to the audit_log MongoDB collection:
     entry_hash_n = SHA-256(prev_hash_{n-1} || eventType || episodeId || timestamp)

   Modifying or deleting any historical log record breaks the cryptographic chain, making database tampering immediately detectable.

16. THREAT MODELING, SECURITY AUDIT & HARDENING GUIDELINES
================================================================================

Security Threat Matrix & Mitigations:
-------------------------------------

Threat 1: Stalking / Location Tracking by Malicious Actors
- Threat: Attacker claims connection request to discover target location.
- Mitigation: SHARP Protocol conceals raw location. Proximity handshake is required before any identity details are revealed.

Threat 2: GPS Location Spoofing
- Threat: Attacker uses software to fake GPS co-location.
- Mitigation: BehavioralRiskEngine detects velocity anomalies. Camera-based physical QR code scan enforces true co-location.

Threat 3: Central Database Compromise
- Threat: Hacker gains full access to central server database.
- Mitigation: Zero-knowledge architecture guarantees database holds only syndromes, hashes, and public keys—no raw coordinates exist to leak.

Threat 4: Man-In-The-Middle (MITM) Interception
- Threat: Eavesdropping on public Wi-Fi networks.
- Mitigation: Enforced TLS 1.3 certificate pinning; TweetNaCl Ed25519 payload signatures at application layer.

Threat 5: Replay Attacks
- Threat: Replaying old peer capsule tokens to claim unearned trust rewards.
- Mitigation: Capsules enforce strict expiration timestamps (expiresAt) and single-use nonces stored in the audit chain.
